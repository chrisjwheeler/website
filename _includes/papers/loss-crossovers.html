Loss Crossovers and Phase Transitions Under Ambient Training in Diffusion Models

Abstract

The distinct behaviours of memorisation and generalisation in generative models has been noted empirically, and linked to sharp phase transitions as model capacity increases. Further, recent work has shown that for the standard diffusion loss objective crossovers between memorising and generalising solutions predict the phase transition location. We investigate how loss crossovers behave in the ambient regime introduced by Shah et al. (2025). Our main result in fig. 2, shows that crossover locations inherit the same bell-shaped dependence on tn demonstrated empirically. All the experiments in our project are run on a rented NVIDIA 4080 Ti with a compute budget of less than Â£10.

1 Introduction

In the study of generative models memorisation refers to behaviour where samples copy images from the training data. In contrast, when a model produces novel samples from the true distribution it is said to have generalised. Recent study has noted a phase shift between memorising and generalising behaviour Buchanan et al. (2025); Wang et al. (2024); Zhang et al. (2023), where once model-capacity passes a threshold in relation to the dataset the shift from memorisation to generalisation occurs suddenly.

(2025) leverage Ambient score matching which is introduced in Daras et al. (2024) to create an algorithm which encourages generalisation in the high noise regime. The authors argue that the images in the noised dataset are harder to memorise as they have been corrupted by Gaussian noise which is incompressible. Further, distinct samples at the zero noise level merge together during the forward process further reducing the incentive for memorisation. Studying the algorithm 1, it is clear that as tn approaches one we recover the typical diffusion training objective, and as tn approaches zero the benefit of the image corruption is reduced. Thus, we would expect any measure of incentive-to-memorise plotted against tn to have an approximate bell shape – we refer to this as the bell-shaped property.

A noteable drawback of algorithm 1 is that is reduces image quality. It is shown in Figure 1 of Shah et al. (2025) that there is trade-off between decreasing memorisation and image quality. 5 The Properties of Loss Crossovers in Ambient Training

Our main finding is that the crossover location as a function of tn exhibits the same bell-shaped structure that we observe empirically; suggesting that crossovers remain predictive in the regime governed by algorithm 1. We begin with an empirical study of the affect that algorithm 1 has on memorisation as tn varies. Figure 1 shows that there is an inverted bell-curve when memorisation ratio is plotted against tn as when tn is close to 0 or 1 the trained model exhibits large amounts of memorisation, but for intermediate tn memorisation is reduced. This suggests that the location of the phase transition from memorisation to generalisation with respect to model capacity would be delayed for intermediate values of tn.

Now, motivated by the relationship between crossovers and phase transitions in Buchanan et al. (2025) we investigate the properties of the crossovers in the regime of algorithm 1. In fig. 2 we demonstrate that the crossovers exhibit the same bell-shaped dependency on tn that we observed empirically. We take this as strong evidence that crossovers could be used to accurately predict the affect that tn has on phase transitions. An exciting prospect for future work is to investigate whether there exists a weight function such that the crossovers predict the location of phase transitions as well as the properties.

We finish our study under the assumption that loss crossovers predict the locations of phase transition. A notable property of the original crossover theorem 1 is that Mâ‹† is linear in N. In fig. 3(left) we plot the crossover point as a function of N and tn in the ambient regime and notice limited variation in N. This suggests that similar to theorem 1 a crossover occurs when M/N is equal to a fixed constant. Together, all of these results suggest that the loss crossovers retain predictive power over the phase shift even in modified training regimes.
